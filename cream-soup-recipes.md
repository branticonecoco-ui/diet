# Blended Vegetable Soup Recipe Book

## Purpose and limitations

These recipes aim to balance **nutrient retention, bioavailability, digestibility, safety and flavour**. No single method maximises every nutrient at the same time: heat can reduce vitamin C or folate, but it also softens the plant matrix and can make carotenoids such as lycopene and beta-carotene more accessible. This is why brief steaming, saucepan cooking, oven roasting, frying-pan cooking and fresh additions are combined.

The times are initial ranges for the cuts specified. Visual and textural doneness, package instructions and the appliance manual take precedence. Power, load, initial temperature and piece size change the result. Nutritional optimisations are reasonable strategies, not guarantees of a specific clinical amount.

Each complete recipe produces **five portions equal by weight**, normally close to 500 ml before the additions. One portion is eaten and four are frozen. The optional recipe is not part of the regular rotation.

Quantities to buy for each recipe, the consolidated quantities for the six regular batches and the weekly fresh shopping are in the [Spanish soup shopping list](lista-compra-cremas.md).

## Weighing and purchasing conventions

- Vegetables: raw weight, cleaned and ready to cook, unless another state is specified.
- Pulses and lentils: weight when already cooked and drained. To simplify preparation and avoid unsafe cooking, use rinsed tinned products or fully cooked products.
- Peas and edamame: cooked and drained weight. Always follow the package instructions; “frozen” or “blanched” does not necessarily mean ready to eat.
- Tofu: drained weight. For it to be considered a reliable source of calcium, the label must state calcium sulphate or another calcium salt.
- Beetroot and artichoke hearts: cooked and drained weight. If tinned, rinse them to reduce sodium.
- Mushrooms: clean weight. If vitamin D is a goal, the label must state UV exposure or vitamin D content.
- Fresh quantities specified “per serving” are bought each week; they are not incorporated into the batch that is frozen.
- Use water or low-salt stock. If salt is added, prefer iodised salt, without assuming that an unmeasured amount covers daily iodine needs.

## Equipment and safety rules

### Kenwood FS620

- Fill the reservoir only with cold water, between **MIN and MAX**. Do not put stock, salt, spices or other liquids in the reservoir.
- Assemble the baskets in order: 1 at the bottom, 2 in the middle and 3 at the top. The manual's times use the bottom basket as their reference; upper levels may need approximately 5–10 additional minutes.
- For repeatable results and short cooking times, cooking in batches in basket 1 is preferable. If several baskets are used, begin checking the texture and extend the time only as necessary.
- The times in this recipe book are counted from the point when stable steam is visible. Allow sufficient leeway and monitor the water level.
- Open the lid away from your face and remove the baskets with protection: steam, condensation and containers can burn.
- Do not use the water from the reservoir. The liquid in the drip tray is not needed for the recipes either.

### Braun PowerBlend 9 JB9040

- The jug has a physical capacity of 3 litres, but the documented general usable capacity is 2 litres and the stated maximum for hot soup is 1.5 litres. Blend in **as many batches as necessary, without exceeding approximately 1.25 litres per batch**. Use two batches only if the measured volume does not exceed 2.5 litres; if it does, use three. Do not fill by estimation or exceed the limit in the regional manual.
- The Hot Soup programme heats through friction and starts with ingredients at room temperature; it does not cook pulses safely or replace the recipes' heating steps.
- To avoid hot liquid under pressure and additional heat exposure, these recipes use manual blending after active cooling. Transfer each finished component to a shallow container, use an ice bath and refrigerate without delay; **do not leave it on the worktop until it reaches room temperature or pour boiling or very hot ingredients into the jug**. Blend only when the contents are below the maximum temperature permitted by the regional manual; if there is any doubt, cool them completely in the fridge. If the manual for the specific unit differs, that manual takes precedence.
- Fully secure the jug, lid and cap. Start at low speed and increase gradually; blend for 30–60 seconds, or until no fibres or lumps remain. Wait until it has stopped completely before opening.
- Do not extend cycles to heat the blended soup. The portion to be eaten is reheated afterwards in a saucepan or microwave.

### Saucepan, oven and frying pan

- **Full boil:** continuous bubbling close to 100 °C at sea level. This is required only in the green bean recipe.
- **Gentle simmer:** small, sustained bubbles without vigorous boiling. As a guide, it may be around 90–95 °C, but altitude and composition change the boiling point, and the visual criterion takes precedence.
- **Oven:** temperatures are given for a conventional oven and, in parentheses, for a fan oven. Use baking parchment or a non-stick surface; the extra-virgin olive oil (EVOO) in the plan is reserved for serving.
- **Frying pan:** medium or medium-high heat, without smoke or blackened surfaces. Cook mushrooms in batches so that they release their water and brown instead of boiling while crowded together.

## Preparation repeated in every recipe

1. Wash your hands and clean surfaces. Wash fruit, vegetables, leaves and mushrooms under running water, without soap; remove damaged parts.
2. First prepare the uniform cuts specified in each recipe.
3. After cutting cruciferous vegetables, let them rest for **approximately 20–30 minutes** while preparing everything else. This is a practical optimisation to support myrosinase activity, not a demonstrated human threshold.
4. Crush or chop the garlic at the beginning and let it rest for **about 10 minutes**. This interval is practical, not a guarantee or a clinically established minimum.
5. Cook only to the specified doneness. Avoid keeping vegetables hot while other batches finish.
6. Transfer each finished component to a shallow container and begin cooling it without waiting for the rest. Use an ice-water bath and refrigerate without delay; count all accumulated time out of refrigeration within the two-hour limit —one hour if the ambient temperature exceeds 32 °C—.
7. When all the components are below the maximum temperature permitted by the Braun manual —or completely cold if there is any doubt—, combine them with the reserved liquid. Measure the volume and blend in as many batches as necessary, without exceeding about 1.25 litres per batch. Add cold water or stock in 50 ml increments only if needed.
8. Combine all the batches, weigh the complete soup base and divide the weight by five. Fresh ingredients specified “per serving” are added afterwards and are not included in that weight.
9. Immediately reheat the day's portion to **74 °C throughout**, stirring. Cool the other four portions and freeze them according to the final protocol.
10. After reheating, wait until the blended soup is no longer boiling and is at a comfortable eating temperature. Then add EVOO, seeds, nuts, citrus, herbs, mustard and fresh vegetables. If the aim is to preserve enzymes from mustard or cruciferous vegetables, adding them below about 60 °C is a practical target, not a universal threshold.

## Quick steaming reference

These ranges have been chosen for small pieces and nutrient retention; they are shorter than some of the manufacturer's general times. Check before the lower limit and extend the time if the centre is still raw or hard.

| Food and cut | Time from stable steam | End point |
|---|---:|---|
| Broccoli, 3 cm florets and tender stem in 5 mm slices | 4–6 min | Bright green; knife enters with slight resistance |
| Cauliflower, 3 cm florets | 6–8 min | Tender but firm; no strong smell or mushy texture |
| Kale without thick stalks, 2–3 cm strips | 3–4 min | Wilted and bright green |
| Turnip greens, leaves in 2–3 cm strips | 3–4 min | Wilted, tender and green; thick stalks require more time |
| Red cabbage, 8–10 mm strips | 5–7 min | Flexible but with some bite |
| Courgette with skin, 2 cm half-moons | 5–7 min | Tender centre, without falling apart |
| Carrot, 8–10 mm slices | 8–12 min | Knife enters with slight resistance |
| Pumpkin, 2 cm dice | 10–15 min | Knife enters easily, without turning it into purée |
| Sweet potato, 2 cm dice | 15–20 min | Centre completely tender |
| Celeriac, 2 cm dice | 15–20 min | Centre completely tender |

## Monday — Green-and-purple cruciferous soup with white beans

### Ingredients for one batch of 5 servings

- 450 g broccoli
- 300 g red cabbage
- 150 g kale
- 350 g courgette with skin
- 250 g leek
- 150 g onion
- 550 g cooked and drained white beans
- 15 g garlic
- 500 ml water or stock initially

### Supermarket alternative —without changing the original recipe—

- If kale is unavailable, replace its 150 g with **150 g usable weight of turnip greens**, preferably leaves and tender stalks. If only leaves are used, steam them for 3–4 minutes from stable steam.
- Do not add kale and turnip greens together: between them, they must provide 150 g in this batch.

### For each serving at serving time

- 10 g EVOO
- 10 g freshly ground flaxseed
- 15–20 ml lemon juice
- 5 g chopped fresh parsley
- 0.5–1 g —approximately ¼ teaspoon— mustard powder

### Processing map

| Ingredient | Cut/state | Equipment | Time and end point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Broccoli | 3 cm florets; rest 20–30 min | Kenwood, basket 1 | 4–6 min; bright green and tender but firm |
| Red cabbage | 8–10 mm strips; rest 20–30 min | Kenwood, basket 1 | 5–7 min; flexible, not soft |
| Kale | Without thick stalks, 2–3 cm strips; rest 20–30 min | Kenwood, basket 1 | 3–4 min; wilted and green |
| Turnip greens, if replacing the kale | Preferably leaves and tender stalks; leaves in 2–3 cm strips; rest 20–30 min | Kenwood, basket 1 | Leaves only: 3–4 min; wilted, tender and green; extend only for thicker stalks |
| Courgette | 2 cm half-moons | Kenwood, basket 1 | 5–7 min; tender, not watery |
| Leek and onion | 1 cm slices/strips | Covered saucepan | 8–10 min at a gentle simmer with 150 ml liquid; translucent, without browning |
| White beans | Already cooked and rinsed | Saucepan | 3–5 min with some of the liquid; hot throughout |
| Garlic | Crushed and rested for about 10 min | Off the heat | Add to the saucepan contents when turning off the heat; mix for 1 min |
| Water or stock | Cold and low in salt | Saucepan and Braun | Use 150 ml with the alliums, the rest with the beans and adjust only in 50 ml increments; record the total |

### Sequence

1. Prepare and rest the cruciferous vegetables; crush the garlic.
2. Cook the leek and onion, covered, with 150 ml water or stock.
3. Steam the red cabbage, courgette, broccoli and kale —or the turnip greens chosen as the alternative—, removing each batch at its end point.
4. Add the beans and remaining liquid to the saucepan for 3–5 minutes. Turn off the heat and add the rested garlic.
5. Cool the saucepan contents and steamed vegetables quickly; blend following the shared method.
6. When serving, reheat to 74 °C, allow it to cool to eating temperature and add EVOO, flaxseed, lemon, parsley and mustard.

### Why it is processed this way

Brief steaming limits contact between the cruciferous vegetables and leaves and the water. Mustard is added after heating as an external source of myrosinase; lemon accompanies the plant iron, and EVOO helps with fat-soluble compounds.

## Tuesday — Mediterranean red-and-orange soup with chickpeas

### Ingredients for one batch of 5 servings

- 700 g ripe tomatoes
- 250 g red pepper for roasting
- 250 g carrot
- 300 g courgette with skin
- 200 g onion
- 550 g cooked and drained chickpeas
- 50 g tomato purée without excess salt
- 15 g garlic
- 300–400 ml water or stock initially
- 4 g sweet paprika
- 0.5 g black pepper

### Theoretical total fresh reserve for the 5 servings — do not buy all at once or freeze

- 100 g fresh red pepper in total: buy 20 g for each weekly serving

### For each serving at serving time

- 20 g fresh red pepper, bought that week, in 5 mm dice
- 10 g EVOO
- 10 g whole-seed tahini
- 10 ml Sherry vinegar
- 5 g chopped fresh basil

### Processing map

| Ingredient | Cut/state | Equipment | Time and end point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tomato | Halves or quarters, cut side up | Oven at 190 °C (180 °C fan) | 20–25 min; collapsed and juicy, with no blackened areas |
| Pepper for the soup base | 3 cm strips | Oven | 18–25 min; tender, not charred |
| Onion | 2–3 cm wedges | Oven | 20–25 min; tender, with lightly browned edges |
| Carrot | 8–10 mm slices | Kenwood, basket 1 | 8–12 min; tender but firm |
| Courgette | 2 cm half-moons | Kenwood, basket 1 | 5–7 min; tender, not falling apart |
| Chickpeas | Already cooked and rinsed | Saucepan | 3–5 min at a gentle simmer with 300 ml liquid; hot throughout |
| Tomato purée | Ready to use, low in salt | Saucepan | Add with the chickpeas; disperse completely and heat for 3–5 min |
| Sweet paprika | Ground | Saucepan | Add with the chickpeas; do not toast or burn |
| Black pepper | Ground | Saucepan | Add with the chickpeas and mix |
| Garlic | Crushed and rested for about 10 min | Off the heat | Add when turning off the heat; mix for 1 min |
| Water or stock | Cold and low in salt | Saucepan and Braun | Use 300 ml in the saucepan and up to 100 ml to adjust in 50 ml increments |
| Fresh pepper | 5 mm dice | No cooking | Add 20 g after reheating each serving |

### Sequence

1. Preheat the oven. Crush the garlic.
2. Roast the tomatoes, 250 g pepper and onion on baking parchment, in a single layer. Remove each ingredient when it reaches its end point.
3. Steam the carrot and courgette in batches.
4. Heat the chickpeas, tomato purée, paprika and black pepper with 300 ml liquid; turn off the heat and add the garlic.
5. Cool all the components, blend and adjust with up to 100 ml extra if needed.
6. When serving, reheat to 74 °C and add the fresh pepper, EVOO, tahini, vinegar and basil when it is no longer boiling.

### Why it is processed this way

Cooked and blended tomato provides a more accessible lycopene matrix; EVOO and tahini provide fat in the same meal. Cooked carrot also releases carotenoids more effectively. Fresh pepper retains vitamin C better than if the entire batch were roasted and reheated.

## Wednesday — High-protein green soup with edamame and tofu

### Ingredients for one batch of 5 servings

- 300 g broccoli
- 200 g kale
- 350 g cooked and drained peas
- 300 g courgette
- 200 g leek
- 500 g fully cooked and drained edamame
- 300 g calcium-set tofu, drained
- 15 g garlic
- 450 ml water or stock initially

### Verified supermarket alternatives —without changing the original recipe—

- If kale is unavailable, replace its 200 g with **200 g usable weight of turnip greens**, preferably leaves and tender stalks. If only leaves are used, steam them for 3–4 minutes from stable steam.
- If watercress is unavailable, replace the 30 g for each serving with **30 g fresh rocket**, added at the same time and without cooking.
- If hemp seeds are unavailable, replace their 15 g with **15 g chopped walnuts** per serving, added without heating.
- Do not combine the original and alternative: kale plus turnip greens must provide 200 g; watercress plus rocket, 30 g; and hemp plus walnuts, 15 g per serving.
- Walnuts are not an exact nutritional equivalent to hemp and provide less protein. The tofu, edamame and peas are not increased as a result: the original soup base keeps its quantities.

### Theoretical total fresh reserve for the 5 servings — do not buy all at once or freeze

- 150 g fresh watercress in total: buy 30 g for each weekly serving

### For each serving at serving time

- 30 g fresh watercress, bought that week, washed and well drained
- 15 g hemp seeds
- 5 g EVOO
- 15 ml lemon juice
- 5 g chopped fresh mint or coriander
- 0.5 g black pepper

### Processing map

| Ingredient | Cut/state | Equipment | Time and end point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Broccoli | 3 cm florets; rest 20–30 min | Kenwood, basket 1 | 4–6 min; bright green and tender but firm |
| Kale | Without thick stalks, 2–3 cm strips; rest 20–30 min | Kenwood, basket 1 | 3–4 min; wilted and green |
| Turnip greens, if replacing the kale | Preferably leaves and tender stalks; leaves in 2–3 cm strips; rest 20–30 min | Kenwood, basket 1 | Leaves only: 3–4 min; wilted, tender and green; extend only for thicker stalks |
| Courgette | 2 cm half-moons | Kenwood, basket 1 | 5–7 min; tender |
| Leek | 1 cm slices | Covered saucepan | 8–10 min at a gentle simmer with 150 ml liquid |
| Peas | Fully cooked according to the package instructions | Saucepan | Heat for 3–5 min; hot throughout |
| Edamame | Fully cooked according to the package instructions | Saucepan | Heat for 3–5 min; hot throughout |
| Tofu | Drained, 3 cm dice; only if the label states ready to eat | No additional cooking | Add cold when blending; if it is not ready to eat, cook it beforehand according to the package instructions |
| Garlic | Crushed and rested for about 10 min | Off the heat | Add when turning off the heat; mix for 1 min |
| Water or stock | Cold and low in salt | Saucepan and Braun | Use 150 ml with the leek, the rest with the peas and edamame, and adjust in 50 ml increments |
| Watercress or rocket, if using the alternative | Fresh leaves | No cooking | Add 30 g after reheating each serving |

### Sequence

1. Confirm that the edamame and peas are fully cooked according to their package instructions. Prepare the broccoli and kale —or the turnip greens chosen as the alternative— and crush the garlic.
2. Cook the leek, covered, with 150 ml liquid.
3. Steam the courgette, broccoli and kale —or turnip greens— in short batches.
4. Heat the peas and edamame with the remaining liquid; turn off the heat and add the garlic.
5. Cool everything. Blend with the ready-to-eat tofu —or tofu cooked beforehand according to its package instructions— and adjust the texture.
6. When serving, reheat to 74 °C; when it is no longer boiling, add watercress or rocket, hemp or chopped walnuts, EVOO, lemon, herbs and pepper.

### Why it is processed this way

The tofu does not need further cooking if the package identifies it as ready to eat. The watercress is bought and added fresh to each serving to avoid freezing and reheating it; it provides vitamin C and a source of myrosinase for the cooked broccoli. Rocket fulfils the same practical function as a fresh alternative when watercress is unavailable. Walnuts replace hemp for availability and texture, but they do not exactly reproduce its nutritional profile or protein contribution.

## Thursday — Orange soup with lentils and UV-exposed mushrooms

### Ingredients for one batch of 5 servings

- 650 g pumpkin
- 300 g sweet potato
- 250 g carrot
- 300 g mushrooms labelled as exposed to UV light
- 550 g cooked and drained lentils
- 200 g onion
- 15 g fresh ginger
- 5 g ground turmeric
- 0.5 g black pepper
- 550 ml water or stock initially

### For each serving at serving time

- 15 g ground pumpkin seeds
- 5 g EVOO
- 15 ml lemon or lime juice
- 5 g chopped fresh coriander

### Processing map

| Ingredient | Cut/state | Equipment | Time and end point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pumpkin | Peeled, 2 cm dice | Kenwood, basket 1 | 10–15 min; knife enters easily |
| Sweet potato | Peeled, 2 cm dice | Kenwood, basket 1 | 15–20 min; centre completely tender |
| Carrot | 8–10 mm slices | Kenwood, basket 1 | 8–12 min; tender but firm |
| Onion | 1 cm strips | Covered saucepan | 8–10 min at a gentle simmer with 150 ml liquid |
| UV-exposed mushrooms | 5–8 mm slices | Frying pan, in 2–3 batches | 8–12 min total per batch; water evaporated and lightly browned |
| Lentils | Already cooked and rinsed | Saucepan | 3–5 min with the remaining liquid; hot throughout |
| Ginger | Peeled and grated | Off the heat | Add when turning off the heat; mix for 1 min |
| Turmeric | Ground | Off the heat | Add when turning off the heat; disperse completely |
| Black pepper | Ground | Off the heat | Add when turning off the heat and mix |
| Water or stock | Cold and low in salt | Saucepan and Braun | Use 150 ml with the onion, the rest with the lentils and adjust in 50 ml increments |

If the mushrooms are shiitake, cook them thoroughly for **at least 20 minutes in total, or for the longer time stated on the package**. For other species, follow their label and use evaporated liquid and a tender texture as the criteria. Do not count vitamin D if the label does not confirm it.

### Sequence

1. Prepare the root vegetables and pumpkin. Steam in batches, beginning with the sweet potato.
2. Cook the onion, covered, with 150 ml liquid.
3. Cook the mushrooms in batches without crowding; do not burn them.
4. Add the lentils and remaining liquid to the saucepan for 3–5 minutes. Turn off the heat and add the ginger, turmeric and pepper.
5. Cool all the components, blend and divide by weight.
6. When serving, reheat to 74 °C and add the seeds, EVOO, citrus and coriander when it is no longer boiling.

### Why it is processed this way

Steaming avoids discarding water from the root vegetables. Cooking and blending support beta-carotene accessibility, and the fats added at serving time help with carotenoid absorption. The mushrooms are cooked separately to ensure thorough cooking without prolonging the heating of the rest.

## Friday — White soup with alliums, mushrooms and cannellini beans

### Ingredients for one batch of 5 servings

- 250 g celeriac
- 500 g cooked and drained cannellini beans
- 150 g onion
- 15 g garlic
- 500 ml water or stock initially
- 2 g dried thyme
- 0.5 g nutmeg

### Theoretical total fresh reserve for the 5 servings — do not buy all at once or freeze

- 100 g fresh rocket in total: buy 20 g for each weekly serving

### For each serving at serving time

- 20 g fresh rocket, bought that week, washed and well drained
- 15 g ground sunflower seeds
- 5 g EVOO
- 15 ml lemon juice
- 2 g chopped fresh thyme

### Processing map

| Ingredient | Cut/state | Equipment | Time and end point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cauliflower | 3 cm florets; rest 20–30 min | Kenwood, basket 1 | 6–8 min; tender but firm |
| Celeriac | Peeled, 2 cm dice | Kenwood, basket 1 | 15–20 min; tender centre |
| Leek and onion | 1 cm slices/strips | Covered saucepan | 8–10 min at a gentle simmer with 150 ml liquid |
| Mushrooms | 5–8 mm slices | Frying pan, in 2–3 batches | 8–12 min per batch for species whose package permits this; if shiitake, at least 20 min or the longer time stated on the package; tender and with no free water |
| Cannellini beans | Already cooked and rinsed | Saucepan | 3–5 min with remaining liquid; hot throughout |
| Garlic | Crushed and rested for about 10 min | Off the heat | Add when turning off the heat; mix for 1 min |
| Dried thyme | Dried leaves | Off the heat | Add when turning off the heat and disperse |
| Nutmeg | Ground | Off the heat | Add when turning off the heat and disperse |

### Sequence

1. Prepare the cauliflower and garlic. Steam the celeriac and then the cauliflower.
2. Cook the leek and onion, covered, with 150 ml liquid.
3. Cook the mushrooms in batches, without overcrowding the frying pan.
4. Add the beans and remaining liquid to the saucepan for 3–5 minutes; turn off the heat and add the garlic, thyme and nutmeg.
5. Cool, combine with the mushrooms and steamed vegetables, blend and divide.
6. When serving, reheat to 74 °C; then add the rocket, seeds, EVOO, lemon and fresh thyme.

### Why it is processed this way

The cauliflower is steamed briefly, and the rocket is eaten fresh, so it does not undergo freezing and reheating. The mushrooms are cooked thoroughly and separately, and the alliums are softened without discarding their liquid.

## Optional — Green soup with artichoke and calcium-rich tofu

| Peas | Already cooked according to the package instructions | Saucepan | Add during the final 3 min |
| Tofu | Drained, 3 cm dice; only if the label states ready to eat | No additional cooking | Add cold when blending; if it is not ready to eat, cook it beforehand according to the package instructions |
| Garlic | Crushed and rested for about 10 min | Off the heat | Add when turning off the heat; mix for 1 min |
| Water or stock | Low in salt | Saucepan and Braun | Use the 550 ml to boil the green beans and retain it in the blended soup; adjust only if needed |
| Romaine lettuce | Very fine strips | No cooking | Add 30 g after reheating each serving |

### Sequence

1. Bring the 550 ml water or stock to a boil. Add the green beans and onion and maintain a continuous boil for at least 10 minutes.
2. Meanwhile, steam the broccoli. Crush the garlic.
3. Add the artichoke and peas to the saucepan during the final minutes according to the table. Do not discard the liquid.
4. Turn off the heat and add the garlic. Cool the saucepan contents and broccoli quickly.
5. Blend with the ready-to-eat tofu —or tofu cooked beforehand according to its package instructions— and divide by weight.
6. When serving, reheat to 74 °C; then add the romaine lettuce, chia, EVOO, lemon, dill, pepper and, if desired, mustard. Eat immediately because the chia will thicken it.

### Why it is processed this way

The green beans receive the required full boil to reduce the risk associated with lectins; the liquid is retained in the blended soup. The broccoli is cooked separately and briefly. The tofu avoids unnecessary initial cooking only when its package identifies it as ready to eat; the romaine lettuce is added fresh, and every soup base is always reheated safely before serving.

## Adjusting texture without losing control of the batch

- If it is too thick: add water or stock in 50 ml increments, blend for 10 seconds and reassess.
- If it is too thin: do not boil it for a long time to reduce it. Add a small additional amount of a compatible cooked pulse, or accept a slightly larger portion and record the change.
- If fibres remain: respect the load limit, start slowly and extend by 15–30 seconds; do not compensate by filling the jug further.
- If it is bitter or very intense: check that there was no charring; adjust when serving with citrus, herbs or a small extra amount of water. Do not conceal spoiled ingredients with seasonings.
- During the pilot phase, record the final liquid used, total weight, weight per portion, actual time for each item of equipment and digestive tolerance. These real data must progressively replace the initial ranges.

## Cooling, freezing, defrosting and serving

### After blending

1. Reheat only the portion that will be eaten that day to 74 °C.
2. Distribute the other four cold or lukewarm portions among individual, shallow, freezer-safe containers, leaving room for expansion.
3. Refrigerate them immediately; the entire process from the end of cooking to refrigeration must not exceed two hours —one hour if the ambient temperature exceeds 32 °C—.
4. Keep the fridge at **4 °C or below** and the freezer at **−18 °C or below**. Freeze the four portions as soon as they are cold.
5. Label with the name, preparation date and weight. Eat them within the four weeks of the planned rotation; this period is conservative and relates to quality, not to freezing sterilising the food.

### Defrosting and reheating

1. Defrost in the fridge or with the appropriate microwave programme. Never leave it on the worktop for hours.
2. If defrosted in the microwave, reheat and eat immediately.
3. Reheat only once, stirring several times until it reaches **74 °C throughout the serving**. In a saucepan, a brief boil at the end helps make the temperature uniform, but do not prolong it.
4. Allow it to cool to eating temperature and then add the fresh ingredients and other additions.
5. Do not refreeze a serving that has remained out of refrigeration for too long or repeat heating and cooling cycles.

## Mandatory pilot phase

Before preparing all six full batches, make one or two recipes with half of each ingredient. Divide the result by weight into five half-portions, not two large portions. Check:

- tolerance of pulses, alliums, cruciferous vegetables and the volume of fibre;
- actual Kenwood times with the load and cuts used;
- saucepan, trays, frying-pan and freezer capacity;
- final liquid needed;
- texture after freezing and reheating;
- intensity of the garlic, mustard, fresh leaves and spices.

If 500 ml is excessive, begin with 300–400 ml and adjust the rest of the dinner according to the plan, without forcing digestive tolerance.

## Nutrients that require another strategy

These blended soups do not by themselves guarantee vitamin B12, iodine, enough vitamin D, EPA/DHA, or all the necessary energy and protein. These points are addressed through breakfasts, lunches and the rest of each dinner in the [Spanish weekly plan](plan-semanal.md). Calcium, vitamin D and iodine remain dependent on actual products and labels.
